01

Vision · 为什么是现在做 RWA

中文

RWA（Real-World Assets）市场过去三年的合规摩擦，集中在一个根本问题上——链下数据源不可独立验证。投资人尽职调查只能看历史财报，托管方只能信任发行人自报，监管侧只能事后审计。这把"信任发行人"这件事变成了整个产品结构最脆弱的一点。

商米的差异化不在金融工程，而在数据采集层。商米作为全球部署量超过 X 万台的支付硬件供应商，每一笔交易在 POS 终端上由设备级硬件密钥实时签名后再上链，把"信任发行人自报"替换成"信任硬件 + 信任数学"。这不是营销叙事，是合规架构上的实质改变。

Core Thesis · 核心洞察 当数据源本身被监管认可的硬件背书（商米 POS 在新加坡是 IMDA 注册的支付终端），RWA 发行的三个传统痛点——尽调成本、二级流通信息对称性、监管审计成本——同时下降。这是过去 RWA 项目都没有的杠杆点。
Before · 传统 RWA
发行人提交 PDF 财报 → 第三方审计 → 投资人凭信任买入 → 链上仅作记账。

数据颗粒度：季度 / 月。可验证性：依赖人证。
After · 商米 POS 背书
POS 硬件签名每笔交易 → 哈希实时上链 → 投资人随时验证经营数据真实性。

数据颗粒度：分钟级 / SKU 级。可验证性：密码学。

在此之上，未来 5 年投资决策正在快速 AI 化。AI 模型读不动 PDF 财报，但能秒级消化结构化的链上数据。这意味着谁掌握了高精度、机器可读、可验证的资产数据流，谁就掌握了下一代投资决策的底层燃料。商米同时具备"数据源"和"分发管道"两个杠杆，是少数能从硬件层切入 RWA 的玩家。

基于此，我们启动 RWA 试点：第一期面向新加坡 Jurong Point 单店，通过 SFA Section 275 私募豁免向合格投资人发行 6 个月期短期债务凭证；第二期扩展至 4 品牌 6-7 店，验证多代币并行发行和合格投资人间合规流通能力。

Regulatory Architecture

English

The structure outlined below reflects our current working assumptions for Phase 1 (single-store issuance, Singapore). Each element is identified for counsel review; we do not assume any item is settled.

3.1 Issuance vehicle

A Singapore private limited company (Pte. Ltd.) is established as a single-purpose issuance vehicle (the "SPV"), bankruptcy-remote from Sunmi and from the underlying merchant. The SPV is the sole legal issuer of the on-chain instrument; it holds the merchant's promissory note as its sole asset and has no other business. Working name: RWA-JRP01 SPV Pte. Ltd.

  • Phase 1 approach: one SPV per issuance.
  • Open for counsel: whether a series-LLC equivalent or umbrella SPV with segregated cells becomes preferable from Phase 2 (multi-merchant, multi-issuance) — see Q3.

3.2 Securities framework

The instrument is structured as a debenture under the Singapore Securities and Futures Act 2001 (SFA). We rely on the small offers / private placement exemptions:

  • SFA s.272A — small offers cap (S$5m / 12 months), if applicable as a fallback.
  • SFA s.274 — institutional investors only.
  • SFA s.275(1) — accredited investors.
  • SFA s.275(1A) — limited offer, <= 50 persons, <= S$5m / 12 months.

Phase 1 issuance (S$200,000 / 6-month tenor) sits comfortably within s.275(1A). Phase 2 (4 brands, 6-7 stores in parallel) is where the 12-month aggregation rules become tight — see Q2.

3.3 Token characterization

The on-chain token is intended to function as a transferable record of the holder's beneficial interest in the underlying SPV debenture, not as the legal instrument itself. The subscription agreement remains the operative contract; the token is the depository entry.

  • Token standard: SPL Token on Solana mainnet (Phase 1 does not require Token-2022 transfer-hook whitelisting because no public secondary market is offered).
  • Working classification: capital markets product under the SFA Second Schedule (debenture sub-category).
  • Explicitly not a digital payment token under the Payment Services Act.

3.4 Investor onboarding

All subscribers must complete (a) Singpass / NRIC identity verification, (b) accredited-investor (AI) self-declaration with supporting evidence under MAS Notice SFA 04-N12, (c) suitability questionnaire, (d) wallet attestation linking the on-chain receiving address to the verified individual.

  • AI status is re-confirmed annually and at every new subscription.
  • Wallet binding is enforced at the platform layer; on-chain transfers to non-bound addresses are not supported during Phase 1.

3.5 Secondary transferability

Phase 1: closed. No secondary market. Investors hold to the 6-month maturity. Hardship transfers, if any, are arranged off-chain between two AI-verified parties with the platform updating the on-chain holding registry — characterized as a private bilateral assignment, not a market.

Phase 2: limited P2P matching. AI-to-AI transfers via on-platform order book. Whether the platform itself constitutes a Recognized Market Operator (RMO) or qualifies for an exemption is a primary open question — see Q6.

3.6 Credit support & remedies

The merchant (operating company) issues a promissory note to the SPV. Credit support stack:

  • Founder personal guarantee (unlimited, joint-and-several where multiple founders).
  • POS device cash-flow lock-up: a portion of daily POS settlement is auto-swept to the SPV's collection account.
  • Sunmi platform expressly disclaims payment liability; this is a debt of the merchant, not of Sunmi.

On default: investors' recourse is to the SPV, then to the merchant under the note, then to the founder under the guarantee. Dispute resolution: Singapore-seated arbitration (SIAC) under the subscription agreement.

3.7 Custody & settlement

Subscription proceeds (USDC) are held by the SPV in a multi-sig wallet (3-of-5; signers include independent director, Sunmi treasury, and an external custodian). Release to the merchant follows a documented draw-down schedule. Coupon and principal payments are pushed back into the same multi-sig and distributed pro-rata to bound investor wallets on scheduled dates.

3.8 Data integrity layer (the differentiator)

Each POS transaction is signed by a hardware key embedded in the Sunmi terminal at manufacture (root-of-trust attested by Sunmi's PKI). Aggregated daily / hourly merchant performance hashes are written on-chain. Investors and counsel can independently verify that on-chain disclosures match the hardware-attested source data.

We treat this layer as operational disclosure infrastructure, not as a regulatory carve-out — i.e., it improves enforcement of existing disclosure obligations rather than displacing them. Counsel's view on whether/how this should be reflected in the offering documents is welcomed (Q1).

04

Open Questions for Counsel

English

The following are the eight questions on which we most need counsel's view, in rough order of bearing on Phase 1 launch. We have working positions on each; we are not seeking validation of those positions but rather the counter-arguments and risks we have not yet identified.

Q1
Should the hardware-attestation layer be expressly disclosed in offering documents, and if so, how do we avoid creating inadvertent reliance liability?
Context. Our value proposition rests on hardware-signed POS data. Disclosing it strengthens the offering narrative but may create an implied warranty as to the accuracy or availability of that data. Investors who later allege data-stream failure could assert reliance on those disclosures. We prefer factual description + clear disclaimers; counsel's view on the right line is sought.
Q2
How should the SFA s.275(1A) S$5m / 12-month aggregation cap be applied across multiple SPVs in Phase 2 (4 brands × 6-7 stores)?
Context. Phase 1 (one SPV, S$200k) is comfortably within the cap. Phase 2 contemplates parallel issuances by separate SPVs, each issuing to overlapping investor pools via a common platform. We need to understand how MAS treats aggregation when (a) issuers are technically separate but (b) the platform, sponsor, and investor base are common. This drives whether we need to gate Phase 2 by issuance windows, by investor pools, or by structural separation.
Q3
Single-purpose SPV per issuance versus an umbrella vehicle with segregated cells — which is preferable from Phase 2 onwards?
Context. One SPV per issuance maximizes bankruptcy remoteness but multiplies setup cost and operational overhead at scale. A series-LLC equivalent (or Singapore VCC sub-fund structure) reduces overhead but raises questions on cross-collateralization and creditor recourse. We have no preference and would like to be guided.
Q4
What is the enforceability of the founder's unlimited personal guarantee in Singapore, and what is the appropriate scope of Sunmi's payment-liability disclaimer?
Context. We rely on a founder personal guarantee as the primary credit-loss mitigant after the SPV's note. We also rely on Sunmi's express disclaimer that it is not a guarantor or paymaster. Both elements need to survive in distress scenarios, including a misrepresentation or unfair-contract-terms challenge. We want a clean opinion on enforceability and disclosure adequacy.
Q5
Is the on-chain SPL Token the security itself, or merely evidentiary of an off-chain subscription right?
Context. Our working position: the subscription agreement is the operative contract; the token is a transferable record. This affects (a) whether transfers require contract assignment formalities, (b) what happens to investor rights if the token is lost or sent to an unrecoverable address, (c) what disclosures are needed at the token level versus the contract level. Counsel's view determines downstream documentation.
Q6
When does our Phase 2 secondary-matching mechanism cross the threshold into Recognized Market Operator (RMO) territory under the SFA?
Context. Phase 1 has no secondary. Phase 2 contemplates AI-to-AI matching on an order book operated by the platform. We assume an exemption (e.g., MAS guidance for limited investor pools or principal-to-principal matching) is achievable, but we need to know the boundary and what design constraints follow — order-book versus RFQ, on-platform settlement versus off, custody location, etc.
Q7
Treatment of cross-border investors — Hong Kong, mainland China, and other jurisdictions — at the subscription and secondary stages.
Context. We expect investor demand from HK and possibly mainland-domiciled HNW. Phase 1 default: SG-resident AIs only. We need a mapped view on (a) which non-SG investors can be admitted under what local-law analysis, (b) whether reverse solicitation is a defensible posture, (c) the marketing-materials constraints that follow.
Q8
Data sovereignty and PDPA — who owns the merchant's POS-derived performance data once it is on-chain, and what disclosures and consents are required?
Context. Merchant performance data flows through Sunmi's hardware and platform, then onto a public chain in hashed and aggregated form. Singapore PDPA implications are non-trivial: who is the data controller, what consents do merchants give, what are investor rights to raw versus aggregated data, what happens to historic on-chain data when a merchant exits. We have a draft consent framework but want a counsel-level review.

Proposed Engagement Scope

English

We have a clear view of what we would like Davis Polk to take on, and where the boundary sits with other workstreams (technical implementation, on-chain audit, accounting). The structure below is a starting point for discussion — happy to be redirected.

Phase A · Pre-issuance (next 6-8 weeks) Engage Now
  • A1. Memo: Singapore SFA classification of the Phase 1 instrument and the s.275(1A) exemption analysis (covering Q1, Q2, Q5).
  • A2. SPV setup: incorporation of RWA-JRP01 SPV Pte. Ltd., constitutional documents, independent director arrangements, and bank/custody account opening support.
  • A3. Drafting of offering documents: information memorandum, subscription agreement, accredited-investor declaration, suitability questionnaire, risk-disclosure schedule.
  • A4. Drafting of credit-support documents: merchant promissory note, founder personal guarantee, POS cash-flow lock-up agreement.
  • A5. Sunmi platform liability review: drafting and stress-testing the disclaimer language across user-facing surfaces (covering Q4).
  • A6. PDPA / data-consent framework review for merchant-side disclosures (covering Q8).
Phase B · Phase 1 → Phase 2 transition (3-6 months out) Plan Now, Engage Later
  • B1. Structural advice on multi-issuance vehicle architecture: per-issuance SPV vs. umbrella with segregated cells (covering Q3).
  • B2. Secondary-market design: RMO threshold analysis, P2P matching mechanism review, custody and settlement implications (covering Q6).
  • B3. Cross-border investor framework: jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction admission analysis, reverse-solicitation posture, marketing-materials constraints (covering Q7).
  • B4. Ongoing regulatory liaison support with MAS where appropriate (no-action letter / informal guidance, if warranted).
Out of scope · Handled by other workstreams Not Requested
  • × Solana smart-contract audit — handled separately by OtterSec (engagement letter signed).
  • × Technical infrastructure — built in-house; Sunmi engineering team owns implementation.
  • × Tax structuring — engaged separately with [tax advisor TBC]; coordination welcomed but not primary scope.
  • × KYC vendor selection — operational decision; counsel input welcomed on AI-verification adequacy only.
Fee structure · Open to Discussion We anticipate Phase A is fixed-fee or capped-fee work given the defined deliverables. Phase B is more naturally hourly with milestone gates. We are not looking for free-of-charge advisory and would prefer a clean engagement letter that allows the project to scale into Phase 2 without re-papering. Happy to discuss an indicative fee envelope at the meeting.
